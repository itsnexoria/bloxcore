Blox Fruits Combos: Notation, Strategy & the Best Community Picks

A good combo is the difference between winning a PvP fight and getting stunlocked. Here's how Blox Fruits combo notation works and where to find combos other players have actually tested.

What a "Combo" Actually Means in Blox Fruits

In Blox Fruits, a combo is a specific sequence of moves — usually mixing a fruit's abilities with a sword or gun's moveset and sometimes fighting-style moves — designed to keep an opponent stunned or airborne so they can't fight back or escape. A well-built combo can take an opponent from full health to zero before they get a single action in. This matters most in PvP (fighting other players) and in certain raids where landing a full combo on a boss window is the difference between a clean clear and a wipe.

Reading Combo Notation

Most Blox Fruits combos are written in shorthand using the game's default keybinds, since typing out full move names for a ten-step combo would be unreadable. The core keys you'll see referenced constantly are:

  • Z, X, C, V — a fruit's four ability slots.
  • M1 — your basic melee attack, sword swing, or gun shot.
  • Sword and gun moves — usually referenced by the weapon's name plus the key, e.g. "Sanguine Art C" or "Dark Blade Z."
  • Directional cues — "aim up," "look up," "360°," or "jump" tell you how to angle a move to actually connect or extend a combo, since many abilities behave differently depending on camera direction.

A typical combo write-up looks like a short list of these steps in order, sometimes with a note about timing — for example, holding a key briefly before releasing it, or firing two moves close enough together that they cancel each other's recovery animation.

Why Timing Matters More Than the Steps

Two players can know the exact same combo on paper and get completely different results, because Blox Fruits combos live and die on timing. Many combos rely on move canceling — using one ability's animation to skip the recovery frames of the move before it, which keeps an opponent stunned continuously instead of giving them a gap to block, dash away, or counterattack. This is almost impossible to convey perfectly in text, which is exactly why watching a video clip of a combo, when one's available, is worth far more than reading the steps alone.

Fruit, Sword, Gun & Race — How Builds Shape Combos

The specific combo that works for you depends heavily on your build. A Dragon fruit main will use completely different combo openers than someone running Dough or Portal, and swapping your sword from something like Yama to a gun-focused weapon can change your entire combo structure. Race also plays a role — certain races have passive movement or damage bonuses that change how tight a combo's timing needs to be. This is why there's no single "best" combo in the game; the best combo is the one that fits your specific fruit, weapon, and race combination.

Finding and Sharing Combos on BloxCore

The Combos page is where BloxCore players post their own combos — tagged by fruit, sword, gun, and difficulty, with an optional video clip and a short written walkthrough of the actual steps. Every combo has an upvote and downvote button, so the ones that actually work in practice rise to the top instead of getting buried under combos that look good on paper but never land. If you've found or built a combo that's working for you, posting it helps the next player skip the trial-and-error you went through.

A Note on Difficulty

Combos on BloxCore are tagged Easy, Medium, Hard, or Legendary based on how forgiving the timing is. If you're newer to PvP, start with Easy or Medium combos that rely on fewer cancels — a simpler combo you can land consistently beats a complicated one you land one time in ten.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Z, X, C, V mean in a combo?

Those are the four ability slots for whichever fruit you have equipped. Combo write-ups use these shorthand letters instead of full move names since a combo can involve ten or more steps.

Why does my combo not land the same way as the guide?

Most Blox Fruits combos rely on precise timing and move-canceling to keep an opponent stunned continuously. Two players can know the same steps and get different results purely based on timing — watching a video clip, when available, helps far more than reading the steps alone.

Do I need a specific build to use a combo?

Yes — combos are built around a specific fruit, sword or gun, and sometimes race combination. A combo designed for one fruit usually won't transfer directly to a different one.

How do I know if a combo actually works?

Check the upvotes on BloxCore's Combos page — combos that get downvoted usually don't hold up in practice, while highly upvoted ones have been tested by more of the community.
