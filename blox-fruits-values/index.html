Blox Fruits Values: How Pricing Actually Works

There's no official Blox Fruits price list — every number you see anywhere online, including here, is community-estimated. Here's how to actually read and use a value list without getting burned.

Why There's No Official Price List

Blox Fruits Corporation (the game's developers) doesn't set trade values — the in-game economy is entirely player-driven. That means every "value list" you find, whether it's a Discord bot, a spreadsheet, or a page like this one, is really just an aggregated snapshot of what the community has been trading a given item for recently. Values can and do move within days of a game update, so treat any number as a guide for opening a negotiation, not a locked-in price.

Physical vs. Permanent Pricing

The single biggest factor in any fruit's value is whether it's physical or permanent. A physical fruit sits in your inventory as an item — it can be dropped, stolen if you die holding it, and traded freely. A permanent fruit is Robux-locked to your account and can never be lost or traded away once eaten, which makes it meaningfully safer to own and therefore worth more. As a rough rule of thumb, permanent value tends to run noticeably higher than physical value for the same fruit — sometimes double, sometimes more for the rarest Mythicals — because buyers are paying for that safety, not just the fruit itself.

Rarity Tiers, from Lowest to Highest

Every fruit in the game sits in one of five tiers: Common, Uncommon, Rare, Legendary, and Mythical. Tier is a reasonable starting signal for value — Mythicals like Dragon, Leopard, and Kitsune are consistently the most sought-after items in the game — but tier alone doesn't tell the whole story. A Legendary fruit that's strong in the current PvP meta can trade for more than a Mythical that's fallen out of favor.

Demand and Trend — the Moving Parts

Two fruits in the same rarity tier can have very different values depending on:

  • Demand — how many people actively want the fruit right now, usually tied to how good it currently is in PvP, grinding, or a popular combo.
  • Trend — whether that demand is climbing, holding steady, or dropping. A fruit trending up after a buff is worth more today than it will likely be worth in a week once supply catches up.

Game updates are the biggest driver of both. When Blox Fruits buffs a fruit's moveset, its value usually spikes within a day or two as everyone rushes to get it; nerfs work the same way in reverse.

Limiteds and Gamepasses

Fruits aren't the only tradeable items — limited-time cosmetics and certain gamepasses hold real trade value too, especially ones tied to events that won't return. Limiteds in particular can hold or gain value over time simply because supply is fixed and no more will ever be obtainable, unlike fruits which stay available (if RNG-gated) indefinitely.

Using Values on BloxCore

When you post or browse a listing on the Trading board or a Services listing, BloxCore shows each item's community value next to it, split by physical and permanent where relevant, so you can sanity-check a trade at a glance before you commit to anything. These values are community-maintained and, like every value list, are a starting point for a conversation — always confirm the current going rate with whoever you're trading with, since values move faster than any list can keep up with.

A Quick Note on Scams

Inflated or fake "value" claims are a common scam tactic — someone insisting their item is worth far more than the community consensus, hoping you don't check. Cross-reference any value claim against what similar items are actually trading for on the Trading board before agreeing to anything, and never rush a trade because someone claims a value is about to change.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where do Blox Fruits values come from?

There's no official price list from the developers — every value is community-estimated based on what items are actually trading for. BloxCore's values are community-maintained and updated as trends shift.

Why is permanent value higher than physical value?

A permanent fruit can never be dropped, stolen, or lost, since it's locked to your account once eaten. Buyers pay extra for that safety, which is why permanent value consistently runs higher than physical value for the same fruit.

Do limiteds hold their value over time?

Often better than fruits do, since limiteds have a fixed supply that will never increase once an event ends. Fruits, by contrast, stay obtainable indefinitely, so their value depends more on current demand and trend.

How accurate are community value lists?

They're a useful starting point, not a guarantee — always confirm the current going rate with whoever you're trading with, since values can shift within days of a game update.
